Links in / out: where a topic comes from and where it leads

The map is split by link direction. On the left are articles that link to this one: the context the topic belongs to. On the right are articles this one links to: its content. Links that go both ways are the strongest ties and sit in their own band on top.

Sample data

Reading the map

  • Links here
    They mention this topic, it does not mention them. Often the bigger picture.
  • Both ways
    They link each other. The closest relatives.
  • Links out
    This article mentions them, they don’t link back. Often details and background.

Each side shows the most-read articles first. The totals show how many links exist in each direction.

What this lens is good at

  • Shows context the article itself never mentions (who cites this topic)
  • “Both ways” is a cheap, strong signal of close relatives
  • Two API calls per map: prop=links and prop=linkshere
  • Works the same on every article and every language

Weak spots to judge

  • Popular topics have thousands of incoming links; ranking decides everything
  • Incoming links from lists, templates and navigation boxes add noise and need filtering
  • Says nothing about what kind of thing a link is; could be combined with the Kinds lens as node colors

Titles are real Wikipedia articles. Link directions, totals and view counts are illustrative sample data in assets/sample-data.js. Tap a label for a preview, tap ⊕ to make it the center. Bold labels have sample data behind them.
