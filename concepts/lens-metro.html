Metro: the threads that run through an article

The article is drawn as a metro line map. The grey line at the top is the article in reading order, one station per chapter. Each colored line is a linked article that comes up in several chapters. It stops wherever that chapter mentions it. You see at a glance which ideas carry the whole article and which appear only once.

Sample data

Reading the map

  • Grey line: the article, one station per chapter, left to right
  • Colored line: a linked article mentioned in two or more chapters
  • Stop: a chapter that mentions it; the thin connector leads up to that chapter
  • Longer line: a thread that runs through more of the article

Tap a chapter name to list its links. Tap a line or its badge for a preview, and ⊕ to make it the center.

What this lens is good at

  • Shows what holds a long article together
  • Keeps the article’s reading order, unlike the other lenses
  • Needs no AI in this form: a line is simply a link that recurs across chapters

Weak spots to judge

  • Short articles have few recurring links, so the map is nearly empty
  • Generic links (a country, a year) recur a lot and need filtering
  • Real themes that use different words in each chapter need AI to spot
  • Recentering jumps from a timeline view to another timeline; it feels less like wandering

Titles are real Wikipedia articles. Chapter names and link placement are simplified sample data in assets/sample-data.js. Bold labels have sample data behind them.
