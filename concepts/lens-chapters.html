Chapters: the article’s own outline as a map

This is how WikiMindMap worked in 2007. Chapters become branches, subchapters become sub-branches, and the links in each part become leaves. Tap a chapter or subchapter name to fold or unfold it, just like the old viewer.

Sample data

Reading the map

  • Thick branch: a chapter, in reading order clockwise from the top right
  • Thin branch: a subchapter
  • Leaf: a link that appears in that part of the text
  • +N: a folded part; tap its name to open it

“See also”, “References” and similar housekeeping chapters are hidden by default.

What this lens is good at

  • Mirrors the article, so the map and the text match one to one
  • Good for understanding and summarising one article
  • Simplest to build: one action=parse call gives sections and links

Weak spots to judge

  • Branches show how editors write (History, Reception…), not what the topic is
  • The same link shows up in several branches: Memory appears three times on Mind map
  • Long articles get deep and wide fast; folding is essential

Titles are real Wikipedia articles. Chapter names and link placement are simplified sample data in assets/sample-data.js. Tap a leaf for a preview, tap ⊕ to make it the center. Bold labels have sample data behind them.
